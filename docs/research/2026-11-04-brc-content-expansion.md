## Primary-source inventory and usable editorial direction

Each source entry below is a concise paraphrase. Proposed patient copy is intentionally narrower than promotional claims on the source pages.

### 1. Biography and philosophy

Source: https://biswaroop.com/about-us/

The official biography identifies Biswaroop Roy Chowdhury as an author, researcher, health educator and creator of the DIP Diet. It describes an engineering background and an honorary doctorate in diabetes and chronic kidney disease. It lists books and links to educational courses. The page emphasizes plant-based nutrition, awareness and daily routines. It also makes disease-reversal, award and credential claims that would need separate verification before appearing as hospital trust signals.

Suggested copy: “Dr Biswaroop Roy Chowdhury is an author and health educator associated with the DIP Diet framework. His published work explores food, daily routines and lifestyle education.”

Use on: About Dr BRC; a restrained homepage introduction. Explain honorary qualifications accurately if displayed; do not imply an MBBS, MD, medical-specialist registration or treating-clinician role from this page alone. Avoid a fixed book count because source pages disagree.

### 2. DIP Diet framework

Source: https://biswaroop.com/dipdiet/

The source expands DIP as Disciplined and Intelligent Person's Diet and describes a whole-food, plant-based framework. It sets out a fruit-focused morning, a two-plate lunch/dinner sequence beginning with raw vegetables, and cooked vegetarian meals. It includes quantities calculated from body weight and meal timing. These are the author's instructions, not a verified universal prescription or evidence that each hospital patient follows the same plan.

Suggested copy: “Explore the DIP Diet: Dr BRC's framework for plant-based eating, meal structure and daily food habits. Ask the hospital team how nutrition guidance is adapted to your assessment and existing care.”

Use on: DIP Diet explainer with a simple food-and-meal illustration. Link to the original for further reading. Do not publish the weight formulas as an interactive self-prescription calculator or advise avoiding prescribed supplements. Do not state that the diet cures diabetes, kidney disease, infection or other illness.

### 3. Circadian approach: source explanation

Source: https://biswaroop.com/product/the-circadian-doctor/

The book description introduces the Circadian Chart as Dr BRC's approach combining circadian-rhythm concepts with Ayurvedic Panchkarma. It is also described as educational material for a certification course. The page establishes the existence and subject of the book, not that its disease claims are proven or that every component is available locally.

Suggested copy: “The Circadian Doctor introduces Dr BRC's Circadian Chart and his approach to organizing care around daily rhythms. Read an introduction, then discuss an appropriate daily plan with the hospital team.”

Use on: Our Circadian Approach; education resource card. Avoid reproducing the promise of removing chronic disease or inviting patients to self-design treatment from a book. A visual daily schedule can show illustrative meal, rest and activity categories once the hospital confirms its actual routine; label the schedule illustrative rather than a treatment protocol.

### 4. Broader protocol inventory, requiring local confirmation

Source: https://biswaroop.com/dr-brc-clinics-hospitals/

The national network page groups its approach under food, gravity, heat/Panchkarma, time/body-clock routines, earthing, D.A.N./humming and neem. It discusses DIP Diet, GRAD, head-down tilt, water immersion and vaso-stimulation. Its text addresses a network and contains broad physiological and disease-management claims.

Use for research vocabulary and questions to the owner, not a ready-made Faridabad service menu. Confirm local availability, clinician responsibility, eligibility, contraindications, equipment, charges and supervision before publishing each therapy. Do not reproduce technical tilt angles, water temperatures or self-treatment instructions. Do not use the network's gallery photos as photographs of this hospital.

Suggested interim copy: “Ask our team about the nutrition and daily-routine guidance available for your care needs.” Add named therapies only after local confirmation.

### 5. Consultation process and preparation

Source: https://biswaroop.com/vopd/

The official national Virtual OPD describes registration, a Diet & Medication form, preliminary video consultation, case review, personalized guidance, a written diet plan, Circadian Chart and one-month follow-up. Standard and premium versions differ in Dr BRC's audio versus direct video involvement. This is a separate online offering. The source explicitly says VOPD does not provide emergency services. The page also contains drug-tapering language and inconsistent report-review/support statements.

Recommended local patient copy: “Contact the Faridabad hospital team to discuss an appointment. Keep your recent medical reports, current medicine list and a brief summary of your health concerns ready. The team will explain assessment, next steps and available follow-up.”

Use on: Plan Your Visit; appointment FAQ. Confirm the hospital's actual booking workflow before promising slots, case-review teams, follow-up duration or Dr BRC attendance. Do not inherit VOPD prices, refund policy, apps, national contacts or emergency policy as local hospital policy. Never promise medicine cessation.

The presence of a paper, institutional name or registered trial is not independent confirmation of the site's broad treatment claims. ## Claims and content boundaries

- Do not repeat source claims of curing diabetes (especially type 1), CKD, cancer, autism, HIV or infections as established hospital facts. Avoid guaranteed reversal, time-bound cures, dialysis avoidance and transplant avoidance promises.
- Do not turn “freedom from doctors, drugs and disease” or “medicine-free” positioning into the hospital's care promise. Any medicine changes must be presented as decisions for the responsible treating clinician, never a visitor instruction or conversion incentive.
- Do not repeat unsupported explanations involving detoxification, body pH, free-electron effects, kidney recovery, autophagy or neem safety as established therapeutic mechanisms.
- Do not infer local emergency care, ICU, dialysis, diagnostics, multispecialty staffing, accreditations, insurance coverage, success rates, discharge outcomes or clinician credentials from 250-bed capacity or national network material.
- The owner has authorized use of the official website as a source. That does not establish that photos of other hospitals show this facility. Genuine local photographs should carry location-specific captions; educational illustrations should be labeled as illustrations.
- VOPD is an external, national online service. If linked, clearly identify the destination rather than silently routing local hospital appointment requests into its registration form.
- Use the hospital's owner-confirmed number everywhere local enquiries are intended. Do not import national numbers, Dynamic Memory Pvt Ltd corporate address or old camp dates from the source site.
